Factoring with a pulse comb

A train of phase-shifted light pulses is added up by interference. The sum is bright only when the trial divisor l divides N, so the bright lines of the spectrum are the factors.

Frequency view, out to infinity

Write u = N/l, so the scanned frequency is proportional to 1/l. The comb’s spectrum is periodic in u with period 1: bright teeth sit at every whole number, with smaller fractional revivals between them. Dashed lines mark where the selected l lands in each period.

Past the last period the pattern repeats exactly, so going to infinite frequency adds no new information: l is a factor exactly when u = N/l falls on a tooth. A real comb has finite bandwidth, so this endless repetition is the mathematical limit, not something hardware reaches.

The physics in three steps

1. A pulse shaper delays pulse m so that its phase at the detector is 2π·m²·N/l. In real experiments the scan over l is done by tuning the optical frequency or the delays.

2. The detector adds the fields of all pulses: A(l) = (1/(M+1)) Σ exp(−2πi·m²·N/l). This is ordinary wave addition, and the page computes exactly this sum.

3. If l divides N, N/l is a whole number, every phase is a multiple of 2π, and all pulses arrive in step (|A|² = 1). Otherwise the phases scatter and the pulses largely cancel.

Limits worth seeing for yourself: with few pulses, ghost factors appear; with phase noise, real peaks sink below the threshold. The pulse count and timing precision must grow with the size of N, so this is a demonstrator for small numbers and no threat to RSA. Similar experiments with femtosecond pulse trains have factored numbers such as 15251.
